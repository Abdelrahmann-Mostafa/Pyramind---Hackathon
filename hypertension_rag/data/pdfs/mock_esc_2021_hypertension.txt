ESC 2021 Guidelines for the management of cardiovascular disease in patients with diabetes

§ 1. Introduction
Blood pressure management is critical in diabetes management. This guideline provides recommendations for screening, diagnosis, and treatment.

§ 2. Screening and Diagnosis
2.1 Hypertension prevalence
Hypertension affects approximately 70% of patients with type 2 diabetes.

2.2 Blood pressure measurement
Office blood pressure should be measured using validated automatic devices.
Recommended frequency: Annual screening for all patients.

§ 3. Risk Stratification
3.1 Cardiovascular risk assessment
Risk categories: Low (<10% 10-year CVD risk), Intermediate (10-20%), High (>20%)

3.2 Target Blood Pressure by Risk Category
- Low risk: Target <140/90 mmHg
- Intermediate risk: Target <130/80 mmHg
- High risk: Target <130/80 mmHg (consider <120/70 mmHg if tolerated)

§ 4. Treatment Recommendations
4.1 Lifestyle modifications (first-line)
- DASH diet
- Weight reduction (target: 5-10% reduction)
- Physical activity: 150 min/week moderate intensity
- Sodium restriction: <5g/day

4.2 Pharmacological treatment
4.2.1 Initial therapy
ACE inhibitors or ARBs are recommended as first-line agents in diabetes.
Calcium channel blockers are alternative first-line agents.
Beta-blockers should be used cautiously in diabetes (metabolic side effects).

4.2.2 Drug combinations
For most patients requiring 2+ drugs: ACE-I/ARB + Calcium channel blocker or Thiazide diuretic

4.3 Special populations
4.3.1 Elderly patients (age ≥65)
Target: <140/90 mmHg (more conservative than younger patients)
Titrate carefully due to orthostatic hypotension risk

4.3.2 Patients with chronic kidney disease
ACE-I or ARB recommended regardless of BP (renal protection)
Target: <120 mmHg systolic if eGFR <30

§ 5. Monitoring
5.1 Follow-up intervals
- After therapy initiation: 2-4 weeks
- After dose adjustment: 2-4 weeks
- Stable patients: Every 3-6 months

5.2 Home blood pressure monitoring
Encouraged for all patients on treatment
Normal: <130/80 mmHg
